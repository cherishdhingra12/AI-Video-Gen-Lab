# STORY.md — how we write a reel

This file is the house standard for **writing**: the hook, the story, the dialogue, the moral. It is
read at call time by the agents that write words (`agents._story()`), and it is the bar the script
critic judges against (`agents.critique_script`). Edit it and the very next run obeys it — the same
contract `WAVELENGTH.md` has for production.

`WAVELENGTH.md` still governs everything else (medium, captions, score, brand, the hard nevers).
This file never overrides it. Where they meet — the moral is a caption card, dialogue is spoken
inside the clip — `WAVELENGTH.md` wins.

The audience: an Indian family scrolling on a phone, sound often off, deciding in about a second and
a half whether to keep watching. Every rule below exists because a reel we shipped broke it.

---

## 1. The hook owns the first 1.5 seconds

The first thing on screen and the first line spoken must open a gap the viewer needs closed. Three
shapes work:

- **A number that does not add up** — "5 बजे सुबह 'Good Morning' भेजते हैं… जवाब कोई नहीं देता।"
- **An object that makes no sense yet** — a jar of unopened biscuit packets, a drawer of handwritten lists.
- **A question about a person, with an image behind it** — not a question about the video.

**Banned openings.** No preface, no greeting, no announcement of what is coming, no summary of the
lesson, no title card before the hook. These are throat-clearing and they cost the reel its reach:

> ✗ "अस्सी की उम्र में मैंने ये बातें सीखीं…" — this announces a list. No image, no question.
> ✗ "80 की उम्र में दादी की लिस्ट में क्या था?" — a contents page, not a curiosity gap.
> ✓ "दादाजी रोज़ सुबह 5 बजे 'Good Morning' भेजते हैं… जवाब कोई नहीं देता।"

The hook is one beat. Do not spend two on it.

**The IDEA is the client.** When the brief names its own hook text, its own moral, or its own beats
— as an owner's brief usually does — those are the client's lines. Use them, and build the story
that earns them; the writer's job is not to substitute its own taste for the client's. The rules
below govern everything the brief leaves free. (This rule exists because the first run of the new
critic spent both its rewrites fighting a brief-mandated hook card — its findings were correct
about the sentence and useless to the reel.)

## 2. A list is not a story

Every beat must be **caused by, or complicate,** the beat before it. The test: if two beats could be
swapped with no loss, they are not beats — they are items, and the reel is a listicle.

- **Required: the turn.** Past the middle, a reversal, a reveal, or a fact that changes what the
  earlier beats meant. A reel with no turn is a montage.
- **Escalation, not repetition.** Four parallel joyful things is a shrug. Send → no reply → worry →
  drive over → the phone was dead → the replies flood in: that is a story, and it pays off the hook.
- The last beat is the one the whole reel was built for. It is never cut, never rushed, and never
  swapped with anything else.

## 3. One concrete thing that survives

A reel is remembered by one object or fact the viewer could point at afterwards. Make it specific and
physical: a name, a date, a count, a brand — a cloth that reads **"आरती & विक्रम – 1984"**, a jar with
exactly **seven** unopened packets.

- **Write the visual cause of the feeling, never the feeling.** Not "वो बहुत अकेले हैं" — an untouched
  plate of food, a chair with one cushion dented, a phone with 41 unanswered messages.
- Abstractions do not survive: "खुशियों भरी जीतें", "प्यार", "अपनापन" are nothing to look at.
- If nothing in the script could be named after watching it, the script has no centre. Add one.

## 4. Dialogue is what a person says out loud

Lines are spoken on camera by a character, to someone who is in the room, with a reason to say *it
now*. That is the whole register test.

- **Everyday Hindi, short clauses, natural Hinglish.** How a grandmother actually talks.
- **Never literary or Sanskritised.** Never an epigram, never a proverb-quote, never a life-lesson
  spoken aloud — the moral belongs on the card (`WAVELENGTH.md` §2), so no character announces it.
- **No forced loanwords.** A word nobody says in that room clangs: "री-सेट" in a line about memories
  is written Hindi, not speech.
- **Silence beats a bad line.** If a beat does not need words, leave it silent rather than fill it.

**The word budget is arithmetic, and it is a rule.** Spoken Hindi runs about **2.2 words a second**
inside a generated clip. A clip is ~10s and carries 6–9s of speech:

| Speech in the shot | Words the line should be |
|---|---|
| 6s | ~13 |
| 7s | ~15 |
| 8s | ~18 |
| 9s | ~20 |

A 5-word line leaves dead air the edit cannot fix. A 40-word line cannot be said in the shot. Count
the words. A reel that speaks everywhere needs roughly **60–85 words of dialogue in total** for
30–40 seconds.

## 5. The moral is earned, one line, and not a question

The last *spoken* line carries the reveal — the meaning lands in the story, out of a character's
mouth. The card then states it plainly.

- **One line. True. Earned by what we just watched.** Not a platitude ("सपनों की कोई उम्र नहीं होती"
  over a reel that never showed a dream being doubted).
- **A card that asks the viewer something is a call to action wearing a moral's clothes.** "पूछिए,
  आपके घर के बुज़ुर्गों की इच्छा क्या है?" is a CTA. It belongs in the caption and the NGO card, not
  in place of the moral.
- If the moral is unsupported by the story — nobody shared anything, nobody doubted anything — the
  story is wrong, not the card.
- **A moral the brief dictates is not yours to replace.** If the story cannot earn it, change the
  STORY until it does (§1, "The IDEA is the client") — that is the work, and it is the whole
  difference between a moral that lands and one that is read out.

## 6. Never the same reel twice

The owner's standing rule: **no two reels share a shape.** The hook form, the beat structure, the
turn type and the moral must all be fresh. The writer is given the previous reels' structures so it
can deliberately avoid them, and the critic fails a script that repeats one.

Reusing a *device* (a card, a silent beat, a montage) is fine. Reusing the *architecture* is not.

## 7. Silence is a tool

A wordless beat — a held look, a sunrise, an object alone in frame — is often the best beat in the
reel, and it is never cut for pace (`WAVELENGTH.md` §5). But a reel with no spoken line anywhere is
not a film, it is a slideshow: at least three beats must speak.

## 8. What you return

One document, in this order, whatever else you invent:

1. **MEDIUM** — the required 3-D animation line (`WAVELENGTH.md` §1).
2. **LOGLINE** — one sentence: who, what they want, what it costs them.
3. **VISUAL STYLE** — palette, look, mood, in a paragraph.
4. **BEATS** — a numbered table: `# | visual action | Hindi line spoken on camera (or *silent*) | seconds`.
5. **MORAL** — the card line, written out.
6. **CTA** — the closing line and the brand, kept separate from the moral.

Write the whole document. A script that stops mid-way is treated as broken and sent back.
